Forces & Motion

10 exam-style questions on speed, acceleration, Newton’s laws, weight and stopping distances. Tap Show answer for full working.

3 Easy4 Medium3 Hard
Q1Easy3 marks

A car travels \(150\,\mathrm{m}\) in \(12\,\mathrm{s}\). Calculate its average speed.

\(v = s/t = 150/12 = 12.5\,\mathrm{m\,s}^{-1}\).

Q2Easy3 marks

Calculate the weight of a \(55\,\mathrm{kg}\) person on Earth. (\(g = 9.8\,\mathrm{N\,kg}^{-1}\).)

\(W = mg = 55\times9.8 = 539\,\mathrm{N}\).

Q3Easy4 marks

State Newton’s first and second laws of motion.

First: an object remains at rest or at constant velocity unless acted on by a resultant force.

Second: resultant force \(F = ma\) (acceleration is proportional to resultant force and inversely proportional to mass).

Q4Medium4 marks

A cyclist accelerates from \(2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s}^{-1}\) to \(8.0\,\mathrm{m\,s}^{-1}\) in \(4.0\,\mathrm{s}\). Calculate the acceleration.

\(a = (v-u)/t = (8-2)/4 = 1.5\,\mathrm{m\,s}^{-2}\).

Q5Medium5 marks

A resultant force of \(24\,\mathrm{N}\) acts on a \(6.0\,\mathrm{kg}\) object. Calculate the acceleration and the distance travelled from rest in \(3.0\,\mathrm{s}\).

\(a = F/m = 24/6 = 4.0\,\mathrm{m\,s}^{-2}\).

\(s = ut + \frac12 at^{2} = 0 + 0.5\times4\times9 = 18\,\mathrm{m}\).

Q6Medium5 marks

A car of mass \(1000\,\mathrm{kg}\) is travelling at \(20\,\mathrm{m\,s}^{-1}\). The brakes provide a constant decelerating force of \(4000\,\mathrm{N}\). Calculate the braking distance.

\(a = F/m = -4000/1000 = -4.0\,\mathrm{m\,s}^{-2}\).

\(v^{2} = u^{2} + 2as \Rightarrow 0 = 400 + 2(-4)s \Rightarrow s = 50\,\mathrm{m}\).

Q7Medium4 marks

Explain the difference between thinking distance and braking distance, and state one factor that affects each.

Thinking distance: distance travelled during the driver’s reaction time (affected by speed, tiredness, alcohol, distractions).

Braking distance: distance travelled while the brakes are applied (affected by speed, road conditions, tyre/brake condition, mass of vehicle).

Q8Hard6 marks

A rocket of mass \(500\,\mathrm{kg}\) produces a thrust of \(8000\,\mathrm{N}\). Calculate the initial acceleration as it lifts off. (\(g = 9.8\,\mathrm{N\,kg}^{-1}\).) Explain why the acceleration increases as fuel is used.

Weight \(= 500\times9.8 = 4900\,\mathrm{N}\). Resultant force upward \(= 8000 - 4900 = 3100\,\mathrm{N}\).

\(a = 3100/500 = 6.2\,\mathrm{m\,s}^{-2}\).

As fuel is used, mass decreases, so for the same thrust the acceleration increases (\(a = F/m\)).

Q9Hard6 marks

A velocity–time graph shows a vehicle accelerating uniformly from rest to \(15\,\mathrm{m\,s}^{-1}\) in \(5\,\mathrm{s}\), then travelling at constant speed for \(10\,\mathrm{s}\), then decelerating uniformly to rest in \(3\,\mathrm{s}\). Calculate the total distance travelled.

Area under graph: triangle \(= 0.5\times5\times15 = 37.5\,\mathrm{m}\); rectangle \(= 15\times10 = 150\,\mathrm{m}\); triangle \(= 0.5\times3\times15 = 22.5\,\mathrm{m}\).

Total \(= 210\,\mathrm{m}\).

Q10Hard5 marks

Two forces of \(12\,\mathrm{N}\) and \(5.0\,\mathrm{N}\) act at right angles to each other on an object. Calculate the magnitude of the resultant force.

Resultant \(= \sqrt{12^{2}+5^{2}} = \sqrt{144+25} = \sqrt{169} = 13\,\mathrm{N}\).
